# Drag selection across page turns

On desktop, select through the last word of the right-hand page and keep holding the primary mouse button. Drag beyond the lower-right edge of the text area and dwell for 500 ms to turn the page. A status hint announces the pending turn and prompts the reader to move into the new page's text. The left-to-right selection on an already visible spread still uses the browser's native selection.

After the first turn, the reader retains source offsets independently of the DOM. The endpoint stays fixed through the animation and while the pointer remains in the old corner. Moving into the new text resumes selection from the original anchor. Re-entering text is required before another automatic turn; a stationary pointer never turns repeatedly. Releasing during the animation keeps the last confirmed endpoint and opens the selection toolbar once the spread settles. Escape cancels the selection and any pending timer. Loss of window focus stops the held gesture.

On phones, long-press text and drag into the lower-right page corner. The selection extends to the page end; dwelling for 500 ms turns one page. The corner stays within the viewport and does not require hitting the last character. Move into the new page's text to keep selecting from the same anchor. Remaining in the corner never triggers repeated turns. The toolbar appears only on release, including release during the animation. Touch cancellation, a second finger, or loss of focus discards the provisional selection; a turn that has already started finishes without reopening the toolbar. Pointer capture belongs to the stable reader surface so replacing the text nodes cannot lose the held finger.

The final selection uses the existing highlight, underline, copy, Ask, and inline-note actions. It creates one annotation with one optional note at the end of the entire passage. Quote text is reconstructed from the source chapter, excluding hidden footnote definitions and user notes. Saved locators survive pagination changes. The current locator model supports one chapter, so crossing a chapter boundary shows an explicit message.

`CrossPageSelection` owns its listeners and timers for the lifetime of a loaded book. Ordinary navigation, a new selection, a modal, book replacement, and disposal clear its state. `TextLayer` supplies projected character hit testing and the lower-right text boundary, so selection follows the displayed page geometry.

Validation: `web/scripts/check-cross-page-selection.mjs` runs against local Vite with a fresh Chromium profile. It covers dwell cancellation, real mouse continuation over one and two turns, no automatic selection/turn while stationary, complete quote/copy, one saved annotation and reload, one inline note, release during animation, Escape, and chapter boundaries. The existing `check-turn-grab.mjs` checks ordinary page/cover gestures and interrupted drags.

`web/scripts/check-phone-cross-selection.mjs` uses real touch events against an isolated local sample to check corner dwell, repeated continuation, exact quote/copy and saved ranges, reload, release during animation, cancellation, second fingers before/during a turn, backward selection within a page, and chapter boundaries. It uses the same `PUPPETEER_CORE`, `CHROME_BIN` and `READER_ORIGIN` environment variables as the phone selection regression.
